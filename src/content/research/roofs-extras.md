# Roofs, gutters, solar, windows and seasonal care: extras

Supporting material for the Homeowner Education Centre: FAQ pairs, a sourced climate fact sheet, and "Did you know" pull-quote facts. Australian English. Source keys in square brackets refer to the source list at the end of this file.

---

## (a) FAQ: 12 question and answer pairs

### 1. How often should I clean my roof in Brisbane or the Redlands?
There's no fixed rule. It depends on shade, nearby trees, roof material and how close you are to the bay. Many roofs only need attention every few years, when streaking, lichen or moss becomes noticeable or gutters keep filling with debris. Coated (restored) roofs are different: Dulux recommends washing its AcraTex roof coatings about every 12 months, and more often in coastal areas. Monier suggests a professional roof inspection roughly every five years. [Dulux-guide] [Monier]

### 2. Can I pressure wash my roof tiles?
It's not recommended. Monier, a major Australian roof-tile manufacturer, strongly advises against using pressure washers to clean roofs. High pressure can damage tile surfaces, coatings and ridge pointing, and force water under laps and flashings. Dulux warns that heavy staining on its roof coatings should never be removed with a high-pressure blaster. A low-pressure method matched to the roof material is the safer choice. [Monier] [Dulux-guide] [Dulux-care]

### 3. Are the black streaks and lichen on my roof doing damage?
Usually they're mostly cosmetic at first. Some manufacturers note that moss and lichen don't eat into tiles, but heavy growth holds moisture, slows run-off and sheds debris into gutters and valleys. It can also hide cracked tiles or failed pointing. Light growth on a sound roof isn't urgent. Heavy growth, blocked gutters or a leak are good reasons to have the roof cleaned and inspected. [Marshalls] [Marley] [Monier]

### 4. Does my Colorbond roof need washing?
Mostly not. BlueScope says surfaces regularly washed by rain, which includes most roof sheeting, don't usually need routine washing. Areas sheltered from rain do: fascias, the underside of eave gutters, wall cladding under eaves, carports and patios. BlueScope recommends washing those at least every six months, or every three months in coastal locations with salt spray, using fresh water and a soft approach. [BlueScope-maintain] [BlueScope-guide]

### 5. When should I clean my gutters in South-East Queensland?
At minimum, once a year in late winter or early spring, before storm season. The Bureau of Meteorology identifies October to December as the highest thunderstorm-risk period for southern Queensland, and Get Ready Queensland advises keeping gutters and downpipes clear of leaves and blockages. Homes under gums or heavy canopy may need cleaning two or more times a year. Monier recommends at least yearly, more often with surrounding trees. [BoM-season] [GRQ-maintenance] [Monier]

### 6. If I install gutter guards, do I still need to clean my gutters?
Yes, though less often. Guards stop most leaves getting in, but fine grit, seeds and pollen can pass through or collect on the mesh, and leaves can mat on top and shed water over the edge in heavy rain. Valleys, box gutters and outlets still need checking. Get Ready Queensland's advice to keep gutters and downpipes clear applies whether or not guards are fitted. [GRQ-maintenance] [BMM]

### 7. Will my insurance cover water damage from blocked gutters?
It depends on your policy and the circumstances. Insurers generally cover sudden events, not damage from poor maintenance. A 2023 Code Governance Committee review found 55 per cent of the denied home claims it examined relied on wear-and-tear or maintenance exclusions. The Insurance Council of Australia recommends clearing gutters, fixing roof damage and keeping records of maintenance work. [CGC] [ICA-Senior] [AFCA-gutter]

### 8. Doesn't rain clean my solar panels?
Often it does, especially in heavy summer falls. Research notes that in high-rainfall locations, rain can keep panels sufficiently clean. But light showers may not count: soiling models used by the IEA's solar program treat rain as cleaning only above a daily threshold, such as 5 mm. Flat-mounted panels, bird droppings, salt and the dry July–September months are where soiling builds up. [EPJ-PV] [IEA-factsheet] [BoM-Bris]

### 9. How much power do dirty solar panels lose?
Usually less than people think, unless soiling is concentrated. An International Energy Agency report summarised in Australian media put typical soiling losses at around 3 to 5 per cent globally, higher in dusty regions. Bird droppings are a bigger concern: a small 2024 study found droppings covering about 30 per cent of a panel cut output by about 35 per cent. Check your monitoring data before paying for a clean. [RenewEconomy-IEA] [Sukkur-2024]

### 10. Can I clean my solar panels with a hose and detergent?
A gentle hose rinse from the ground, early in the morning, is generally fine. Avoid detergents unless your panel manual allows a specific product. Manufacturers such as Jinko warn against high-pressure cleaning, Trina advises against alkaline or strong acid solvents, and Trina says damage from inappropriate cleaning can void its warranty. Deionised water and a soft brush are the standard recommendation. Never spray cold water on hot panels. [Jinko] [Trina] [Midsummer]

### 11. Why do my windows have white spots that won't come off?
They're usually mineral deposits from sprinklers or hard water. Glass maker Pilkington explains these start as surface deposits but, if they stay damp against the glass for long periods, can stain or etch it. Abrasive or strong acid cleaners may help once but, repeated, will damage the glass. Redirect sprinklers away from windows and clean fresh marks early. Etching is in the glass and can't be cleaned off. [Pilkington]

### 12. Are water-fed poles as good as traditional window cleaning?
For exterior glass, they're a well-established alternative. A water-fed pole uses purified water, which has its minerals removed so it dries without spots, with no squeegee or detergent needed. The UK's Health and Safety Executive names water-fed poles as a way to avoid working at height when cleaning upper-floor windows. Hand cleaning with a squeegee still gives an excellent finish on glass that's easy to reach. [HSE-window] [DesigningBuildings]

### Key facts

- **Mean annual rainfall:** 1,065.2 mm at Brisbane (040913, 1999–2026). About 1,271.6 mm at Redlands HRS (040265, 1899–2013).
- **Range:** At Brisbane (040913), the highest annual total on record is 1,950.0 mm and the lowest is 652.4 mm.
- **Wettest months:** February (176.1 mm), then March (149.2 mm), January (140.0 mm) and December (137.9 mm) at Brisbane. At Redlands HRS, February averages about 164.1 mm and March about 161.9 mm.
- **Driest months:** September (28.6 mm), July (31.1 mm) and August (34.2 mm) at Brisbane.
- **Seasonality:** About 66 per cent of Brisbane's mean annual rainfall falls from November to March (705.0 of 1,065.2 mm, calculated from the monthly means above).
- **Rain days:** A mean of 83.1 days a year with at least 1 mm at Brisbane. March has the most (10.0) and September the fewest (3.7).
- **Humidity:**
  - Brisbane (040913, 1999–2010): mean 9 am relative humidity about 63 per cent annually, and mean 3 pm relative humidity about 52 per cent. Afternoon humidity is lowest in late winter.
  - Redlands HRS (040265, 1953–2010): mean 9 am relative humidity of 72 per cent in February and 73 per cent in March.
- **Storm season:** The Bureau of Meteorology describes October to April as Australia's peak period for severe weather, including severe thunderstorms. It says the highest thunderstorm risk on the east coast is in northern New South Wales and southern Queensland during October to December. [BoM-season] Queensland Government disaster guidance advises preparing the home before storm, cyclone and monsoon season. [QG-prepare]

Verify monthly humidity values against the live table before publishing anything more specific than the annual means.

## (c) "Did you know" facts for pull-quotes

1. **February is Brisbane's wettest month,** averaging 176.1 mm of rain. That's about six times September's 28.6 mm. *Source: [BoM-Bris]*
2. **About two-thirds of Brisbane's yearly rain falls from November to March.** *Source: [BoM-Bris] (calculated from monthly means)*
3. **More than 1,000 Queenslanders are hospitalised each year after falling off ladders,** and most of these injuries happen around the home. *Source: [QG-ladders]*
4. **In a University of Queensland study of ladder-fall patients, men over 55 made up more than half of cases,** and patients spent an average of five days in hospital. *Source: [UQ-ladders]*
5. **Monier, a major Australian roof-tile manufacturer, "strongly advises against the use of pressure washers for roof cleaning".** *Source: [Monier]*
6. **The lichens on concrete roof tiles are the same species that grow on limestone.** To them, limy concrete is no different from rock. *Source: [ANBG]*
7. **Most COLORBOND® roof sheeting is cleaned by rain.** It's the sheltered spots, such as fascias, eaves and gutter undersides, that BlueScope says need washing every six months, or every three months near the coast. *Source: [BlueScope-maintain]*
8. **BlueScope classes homes within 100 metres of calm salt water, such as a bay, as "severe marine" locations.** *Source: [SteelSelect]*
9. **Soiling models used by the IEA's solar research program treat rain as "cleaning" only above a daily threshold,** such as more than 5 mm in one day. *Source: [IEA-factsheet]*
10. **Sprinkler marks on glass start as a surface deposit, not a stain.** Pilkington warns that if they stay damp against the glass for long periods, they can etch it. *Source: [Pilkington]*
11. **A University of Queensland study found the best fixed angle for Brisbane solar panels is about 26°, facing true north.** *Source: [UQ-tilt]*
12. **More than half of the denied home insurance claims reviewed by the Code Governance Committee in 2023 relied on wear-and-tear or maintenance exclusions.** *Source: [CGC]*
