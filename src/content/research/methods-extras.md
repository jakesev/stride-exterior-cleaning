# Methods and surfaces: FAQs, surface matrix and "Did you know" facts

Companion material for the Homeowner Education Centre articles on cleaning methods and surfaces. Every answer and fact below is drawn from the sources listed under it. Where no reliable source gave a figure, the text says "varies" rather than inventing one.

Note on question selection: the questions reflect common "People also ask"-style phrasing and the concerns that come up repeatedly in Australian renovation and homeowner forums (Bunnings Workshop threads, contractor Q&A). Reddit and Whirlpool threads could not be reached directly during research, so the questions are modelled on recurring search queries rather than quoted from specific threads.

## (a) FAQs

### 1. Is soft washing the same as pressure washing?

No. Pressure washing uses the force of high-pressure water to remove dirt. Soft washing applies a cleaning solution at low pressure, leaves it to work for a set time, then rinses it off gently. Soft washing suits surfaces that force can damage, such as render, painted weatherboard, fibre cement and roof coatings. Pressure washing, set to suit the slab, is generally used on sound concrete.

*Sources: [Soft washing — Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Soft_washing); [Maintenance — Washing (Code of Practice) — NZ Metal Roofing Manufacturers](https://metalroofing.org.nz/cop/maintenance/washing)*

### 2. Can I pressure wash my rendered walls?

Only gently, and only if the render is sound. Rockcote's maintenance guide specifies about 500 psi through a fan tip, not a needlepoint jet, which it warns can lift even the sturdiest finish. Natural lime and clay renders are more delicate. Rockcote says to avoid high-pressure sprays and bleaches on those altogether. Check for cracking or flaking before any water goes on.

*Sources: [Rockcote Maintenance Guide — Rockcote](https://rockcote.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Rockcote-Maintenance-Guide.pdf); [Natural Materials Maintenance Guide — Rockcote](https://rockcote.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Natural-Materials-Maintenance-Guide.pdf)*

### 3. Will pressure washing damage my roof tiles?

It can. Monier strongly advises against using pressure washers to clean roofs. It also notes that walking on tiles risks damage, and recommends leaving roof work to a qualified professional because of the fall risk. Coated roofs have their own limits: Dulux's Acratex roof membrane guide allows less than 450 psi through a fan jet held at least 30 cm away.

*Sources: [Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier](https://www.monier.com.au/roof-cleaning-and-maintenance/); [Acratex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux](https://vivifyroofing.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dulux-Acratex-Roofing-Maintenance-Guide_PFO_LR.pdf)*

### 4. What are the black spots on my driveway?

Usually organic growth such as algae, mould or lichen rather than dirt. These grow best in damp, shaded, poorly drained spots, which is why they cluster under trees and along shaded sides of the house. The Royal Horticultural Society notes they don't damage the surface, but they do make it slippery. Lichen is generally the most stubborn and can take more than one treatment.

*Sources: [Algae, lichens, liverworts and moss on hard surfaces — RHS](https://rhs.org.uk/advice/profile?PID=418); [What causes those black spots on concrete driveways — SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/2264119/what-are-those-black-spots-on-concrete-driveway-how-to-remove-them/)*

### 5. Why does the mould come back after cleaning?

Cleaning removes the growth, but not the shade and moisture that let it grow. Discussing lichen on cladding, the NZ Metal Roofing code of practice warns that "recolonisation is very likely", especially in sheltered and shady areas. Trimming overhanging branches, improving drainage and keeping debris swept away slow regrowth. On decks, the Queensland Government suggests removing plants that permanently shade the surface.

*Sources: [Maintenance — Washing — NZ Metal Roofing Manufacturers](https://metalroofing.org.nz/cop/maintenance/washing); [Maintaining decks and balconies — Queensland Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/buying-owning-home/maintenance-modifications/decks-balconies/maintaining)*

### 6. Can oil stains be removed from a concrete driveway?

Often partly. Concrete is porous, so oil soaks in, and speed matters. Fresh spills respond much better than old ones. For older stains, a poultice (an absorbent paste left on the stain to draw oil out) is commonly used, sometimes over several rounds. Deep, long-standing oil stains usually lighten rather than disappear completely.

*Sources: [How to remove stains from concrete — The Home Depot](https://www.homedepot.com/c/ab/how-to-remove-stains-from-concrete/9ba683603be9fa5395fab9015dcc2c07); [How to remove oil stains — Concrete Network](https://concretenetwork.com/fix-concrete-countertops/remove-oil-stains.html)*

### 7. What is the white powder on my pavers, and how do I get rid of it?

It's usually efflorescence: salts carried to the surface by moisture and left behind as the water evaporates. BRANZ notes it doesn't affect the performance of concrete or masonry. Don't hose it, because water re-dissolves the salt and it returns as the surface dries. Think Brick Australia recommends brushing it off with a stiff, dry brush once the surface has dried.

*Sources: [BU593 Efflorescence — BRANZ](https://www.branz.co.nz/pubs/bulletins/bu593/); [Manual 13: Clay Masonry Cleaning Manual — Think Brick Australia](https://littlehamptonbrick.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/manual-13-clay-masonry-cleaning-manual.pdf)*

### 8. Should I seal my driveway after it's been cleaned?

It depends on the surface and your priorities. Sealers can slow staining, help hold exposed-aggregate stones in place and refresh coloured concrete. They also add a reapplication cycle (Concrete Decor puts acrylic-sealed exposed aggregate at roughly 12–36 months), can make surfaces slipperier, and poorly bonded film sealers can lift under hot tyres. Only seal a clean, fully dry surface.

*Sources: [Seal exposed aggregate concrete for best results — Concrete Decor](https://www.concretedecor.net/departments/concrete-sealers/seal-exposed-aggregate-concrete-for-best-results/); [Choosing decorative concrete sealers — For Construction Pros](https://www.forconstructionpros.com/concrete/decorative/article/10116993/choosing-decorative-concrete-sealers); [What causes hot-tire pickup? — Concrete Decor](https://www.concretedecor.net/departments/stamping-texturing-concrete/what-causes-hot-tire-pickup/)*

### 9. Can I use vinegar or acid cleaners on travertine or limestone?

No. Travertine, limestone and marble are calcareous stones, made mainly of calcium carbonate, and acids dissolve it. Even mild acids like vinegar can etch the surface, and sealing doesn't prevent etching. Use a pH-neutral cleaner made for natural stone, a soft brush and low-pressure water. If you're not sure what your stone is, treat it as acid-sensitive.

*Sources: [Protecting your investment in Mother Nature with TLC — TileLetter](https://www.tileletter.com/protecting-your-investment-in-mother-nature-with-tlc/); [What stones are most prone to etching — Slabwise](https://slabwise.com/guides/what-stones-are-most-prone-to-etching-and-how-to-explain-this); [The dos and don'ts for sandstone — Sandstone World](https://www.sandstoneworld.com.au/blog/the-dos-donts-for-sandstone)*

### 10. Is it OK to pressure wash a timber deck?

It's risky. High pressure, narrow tips and close spraying can strip soft wood between the grain, leaving a fuzzy "furred" surface or permanent marks that need sanding. If pressure is used, keep it low, use a wide fan tip, keep your distance and work with the grain. A deck cleaner and stiff brush with a gentle rinse is usually safer. The Queensland Government recommends sweeping instead of hosing where possible.

*Sources: [How to power wash a deck without damaging your wood — Olympic](https://olympic.com/advice/power-washing-tips); [Pressure-washing a deck — HGTV](https://www.hgtv.com/remodel/outdoors/pressure-washing-a-deck); [Maintaining decks and balconies — Queensland Government](https://www.qld.gov.au/housing/buying-owning-home/maintenance-modifications/decks-balconies/maintaining)*

### 11. How often should I re-oil my deck in Queensland?

On condition, not by the calendar. Cabot's suggests inspecting every 12 months for loss of colour or water repellency. Intergrain recommends recoating before the coating breaks down, and three coats in full sun, near pools or in humid areas. Rules of thumb vary: RACV suggests every six to eight months for oil-based coatings. If water soaks in instead of beading, it's time.

*Sources: [Natural Decking Oil — Cabot's](https://cabots.com.au/product/exterior/oil/); [UltraDeck Timber Decking Oil — Intergrain (Bunnings listing)](https://www.bunnings.com.au/intergrain-10l-natural-ultradeck-timber-decking-oil_p1520075); [Deck cleaning, restoration and repair — RACV](https://www.racv.com.au/royalauto/property/maintenance/deck-cleaning-restoration-repair-how-to-guide.html); [Tips and tricks for timber deck maintenance — Canterbury Timbers](https://canterburytimbers.com.au/blog/tips-tricks-for-timber-deck-maintenance)*

### 12. Will house washing chemicals hurt my plants, pets or rainwater tank?

They can if no one plans for them. Sodium hypochlorite, common in soft-wash solutions, can damage plants and is hazardous to eyes and skin. Plants should be wetted before, during and after, and pets kept inside until surfaces are rinsed. Disconnect downpipes feeding a tank before roofs or gutters are washed, as BlueScope and Dulux advise, and keep runoff out of stormwater drains.

*Sources: [Sodium Hypochlorite Safety Data Sheet — Dalcon Hygiene](https://s3.amazonaws.com/zcom-media/sites/a0i0L00000VH4TSQA1/media/mediamanager/Sodium-Hypochlorite-Dalcon-Hygiene-SDS.pdf); [How to minimise plant damage when using sodium hypochlorite — Pure Seal Services](https://puresealservices.co.uk/how-to-minimise-plant-damage-when-using-sh/); [Home Improvement Maintenance Guide — BlueScope](https://app01.banzcwt.steel.com.au/~/media/Files/Build%20and%20Construction%20Technical%20Library/Documents/HomeImprovementMaintenanceGuideNov2012.ashx); [Acratex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux](https://vivifyroofing.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dulux-Acratex-Roofing-Maintenance-Guide_PFO_LR.pdf); [Water pollution — Redland City Council](https://redland.qld.gov.au/info/20252/pollution_noise_and_nuisances/616/water_pollution)*

## (b) Surface matrix

"Recleaning interval" lists a figure only where a manufacturer or authority gives one; many of these refer to light maintenance washing, not a full professional clean. Otherwise it says "varies" (driven by shade, tree cover, drainage and coastal exposure).

| Surface | Recommended method | Typical risks | Aftercare | Typical recleaning interval in SEQ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plain / broom-finished concrete | Rotary surface cleaner at a pressure suited to the slab; spot treatment for oil and rust | Etching or marks from excess pressure or a close nozzle; old oil and rust only lighten | Sweep regularly; treat spills quickly; optional penetrating sealer | Varies |
| Exposed aggregate concrete | Surface cleaner at moderate pressure; care at edges and joints | Loosened stones if surface paste is eroded | Film-forming acrylic sealer helps hold stones (penetrating sealers don't) | Varies (acrylic reseal roughly 12–36 months, Concrete Decor) |
| Stencilled / coloured / spray-on coated concrete | Assess the coating first; lower pressure; mild cleaner | Degraded coating or old sealer lifting or cleaning unevenly; hot tyre pickup on sealed surfaces | Reseal on condition with a compatible product | Varies |
| Concrete pavers | Surface cleaner at suitable pressure; no hydrochloric acid | Joint sand washout; acid damage to surface | Re-sand joints after cleaning; dry-brush efflorescence | Varies |
| Clay pavers | Low to moderate pressure; identify stains before any acid (Think Brick Manual 13) | Joint sand washout; acid reacting badly with some stains (e.g. vanadium) | Re-sand joints; dry-brush efflorescence | Varies |
| Sandstone | Low pressure only; non-acidic, stone-specific cleaner | Erosion under high pressure; acid, alkaline and bleach damage; some sandstones turn orange with acid | Penetrating stone sealer, highly recommended near salt water (Gosford Quarries); don't seal wet stone | Varies |

| Coated (membrane) roofs | Less than 450 psi, cold-water fan jet at 45°, at least 30 cm away; soft broom and mild cleaner (Dulux Acratex) | Membrane damage from high pressure, harsh chemicals, bleach or sugar soap | Disconnect tanks; rinse with potable water before reconnecting | Every 12 months, more often on the coast (Dulux Acratex) |
| Timber decks | Sweep; timber cleaner, stiff brush along the grain, gentle rinse | Furring, gouging and wand marks from high pressure; tannin bleed from new hardwood | Recoat on condition; three coats in full sun, near pools or in humid areas (Intergrain) | Inspect every 12 months (Cabot's; Queensland Government); recoat varies with exposure |
| Timber fences | As for decks; protect garden beds at the base | Furring; runoff into garden beds; uneven colour on treated pine | Let new palings weather about 6–12 weeks before first coat (Bunnings) | Varies |

## (c) "Did you know" facts

1. **Most Colorbond roofs wash themselves.** BlueScope says rain-washed surfaces such as roof sheeting don't usually need routine washing. It's the sheltered spots, like eaves, fascias and garage doors, that need a wash at least every six months, or every three months near the coast.
   *Source: [Maintain Colorbond steel and Zincalume steel — BlueScope](https://www.bluescopesteel.com.au/howto/maintain-colorbond-steel-and-zincalume-steel)*

2. **One roof tile maker says no pressure washers, full stop.** Monier "strongly advises against the use of pressure washers for roof cleaning."
   *Source: [Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier](https://www.monier.com.au/roof-cleaning-and-maintenance/)*

3. **The nozzle matters as much as the pressure.** Rockcote warns that a direct needlepoint jet "may lift the sturdiest finish" on render. It specifies a fan tip at about 500 psi.
   *Source: [Rockcote Maintenance Guide — Rockcote](https://rockcote.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Rockcote-Maintenance-Guide.pdf)*

4. **Bleach can harm a roof coating.** Dulux's Acratex roof membrane guide warns that bleach or sugar soap may damage the membrane film, and caps cleaning pressure at under 450 psi.
   *Source: [Acratex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux](https://vivifyroofing.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Dulux-Acratex-Roofing-Maintenance-Guide_PFO_LR.pdf)*

5. **Hosing white bloom makes it come back.** Efflorescence is soluble salt. Water re-dissolves it, and it returns as the surface dries. Think Brick Australia recommends a stiff, dry brush instead.
   *Source: [Manual 13: Clay Masonry Cleaning Manual — Think Brick Australia](https://littlehamptonbrick.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/manual-13-clay-masonry-cleaning-manual.pdf)*

6. **Sealing won't stop acid etching.** On travertine, limestone and marble, acids dissolve the calcium carbonate in the stone itself, so a sealer doesn't protect against etching.
   *Source: [What stones are most prone to etching — Slabwise](https://www.slabwise.com/guides/what-stones-are-most-prone-to-etching-and-how-to-explain-this)*

7. **Queensland pool chlorine can come in the "acid" colour.** The NSW EPA notes that pool chlorine is traditionally sold in light blue containers and acid in black, but chlorine sourced from Queensland comes in black containers. No regulation sets container colours.
   *Source: [Colour-coded containers for pool chemicals — NSW EPA](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-02/26p4658-colour-coded-containers-for-pool-chemicals.pdf)*

8. **Pool surrounds have their own slip-resistance guidance.** Industry summaries of Standards Australia's HB 198 list P4 (pendulum test) and B (wet-barefoot test) as guidance for swimming pool surrounds.
   *Source: [Understanding slip resistance — ACO Australia](https://acodrain.com.au/understanding-slip-resistance/)*

9. **Grey timber is sun-damaged wood fibre.** UV light breaks down lignin, the natural binder near the wood's surface. Rain washes it away, leaving loose cellulose fibres that weather to silver-grey.
   *Source: [What causes wood to turn gray outdoors? — Woodworking Network](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/knowledge-center/wood-dr/what-causes-wood-turn-gray-outdoors)*

10. **Driveway wash water is regulated.** Redland City Council includes wastewater from outdoor cleaning among contaminants that must be kept out of stormwater, and Brisbane City Council can issue on-the-spot fines for cleaning runoff.
    *Sources: [Water pollution — Redland City Council](https://redland.qld.gov.au/info/20252/pollution_noise_and_nuisances/616/water_pollution); [Stormwater pollution, runoff and drainage complaints — Brisbane City Council](https://brisbane.qld.gov.au/laws-permits/complaints-fines/noise-air-light-water-complaints/stormwater-pollution-runoff-drainage-complaints)*
