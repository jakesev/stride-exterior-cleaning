# Local area profiles: Redlands and Brisbane bayside

Research notes for service-area pages. All census figures are from the ABS 2021 Census QuickStats ("All persons"). Where a suburb-level (SAL) page was not available in research, the Statistical Area Level 2 (SA2) page is used and labelled; SA2 boundaries can differ from the gazetted suburb. Percentages for dwelling structure are of occupied private dwellings. Queensland comparison figures (2021): median age 38; separate houses 74.8% of occupied private dwellings.

**Shared climate note (Redlands profiles):** Bureau of Meteorology records for the Redlands HRS station (040265) give a mean annual rainfall of about 1,272 mm, with January–March the wettest months (about 160 mm each) and September the driest (about 38 mm), and about 47 days a year with 1 mm or more of rain. Source: [BoM climate statistics, Redlands HRS](https://www.bom.gov.au/climate/averages/tables/cw_040265.shtml).

**Shared salt-air note (bayside profiles):** Corrosion guidance based on AS/NZS 2312 notes that marine salt deposition is heaviest close to the shoreline and falls away inland, with the most severe zone extending only a short distance inland around sheltered bays (it cites up to about 50 m) compared with surf coasts; the extent depends on wind and topography. Salt on sheltered surfaces that rain doesn't reach (such as under eaves) is not washed off. Moreton Bay is a sheltered bay. Sources: [Dulux Protective Coatings AS/NZS 2312.1 guide](https://www.duluxprotectivecoatings.com.au/media/cuvb4eds/as2312-1-2014-brochure-web.pdf); [OSTI abstract on marine atmospheric corrosion](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/684446); [DTIC report on marine atmospheres](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA153843.pdf).

**Postcodes:** Postcodes are standard Australia Post assignments; confirm via [Australia Post postcode search](https://auspost.com.au/postcode) (not re-checked in this session's research).

## Redland Bay (QLD 4165)

Redland Bay sits on the shore of southern Moreton Bay, so bayside salt air is a factor for homes close to the water. The township was subdivided into one-acre lots in 1866 and was long a farming district; Council later allowed suburban development, and rural land was rezoned for housing. Weinam Creek, the ferry terminal for the Southern Moreton Bay Islands, was declared a 42-hectare Priority Development Area in 2013. In 2021 the suburb had 6,268 private dwellings, and separate houses made up 94.3% of occupied dwellings (5,469), well above the Queensland figure. The median age was 40. Summer is the wet season, with January to March averaging around 160 mm of rain a month at the Redlands station.

## Victoria Point (QLD 4165)

Victoria Point is a bayside suburb facing Coochiemudlo Island, and the point provides for a ferry service to the island. Its fertile red soil drew farmers from the late 1800s, and the population grew from around 600 in the mid-1950s to about 2,500 by 1980 before later suburban expansion. In 1990 the then Shire Council acquired 85 hectares of bushland and wetland on Point Halloran, north of Victoria Point, for conservation after it was proposed as a canal estate; there is also an egret colony wetland nearby. In 2021 there were 5,802 occupied private dwellings, and separate houses made up 80.8% (4,690), with a mix of other dwelling types. Waterfront homes are exposed to salt air from Moreton Bay.

## Cleveland (QLD 4163)

Cleveland sits on a headland in Moreton Bay, with the 1864 Cleveland Point lighthouse at its tip. Raby Bay, one of the city's two canal estates, was built on reclaimed foreshore: the first stage opened in 1984 and the last canal was completed in 1997. Cleveland has more varied housing than most Redlands suburbs. In 2021 it had 7,260 private dwellings; separate houses made up 60.4% of occupied dwellings (3,981) and flats or apartments 19.5% (1,285). The average household size was 2.3 people, below the state average. Waterfront and canal-front homes are close to salt air.

## Thornlands (QLD 4164)

Thornlands was a fruit-growing district, known for pineapples and strawberries, before urban settlement began in the 1960s. Its population grew nearly eight-fold between 1966 and 1986, and Redland City Council now describes it as one of the fastest-growing suburbs on the Redlands Coast. That growth shows in the housing: a younger population (median age 36 in 2021) and larger households (2.9 people on average). The 2021 Census recorded 6,779 private dwellings, with separate houses making up 87.3% of occupied dwellings (5,593, SA2 figure).

## Capalaba (QLD 4157)

Capalaba sits inland on Tingalpa Creek, at the western gateway to the Redlands. The Leslie Harrison Dam, completed in 1968, created a major water supply for the then Shire and was followed by strong residential, commercial and industrial growth in the 1970s; the first residential subdivisions date from 1949. Bushland corridors run through the suburb: Council and the State rehabilitated a former turf farm on Smith Street as koala habitat linked to the Coolnwynpin Creek corridor, and Ferntree Park was designated a koala area in 2016. In 2021 Capalaba had 18,002 residents and 7,221 private dwellings (6,716 occupied), with an average household of 2.6 people.

## Alexandra Hills (QLD 4161)

Alexandra Hills was planned in the 1960s as a satellite town on both sides of Finucane Road. In 1967 most of it was still bush, but by 1976 it was the fastest-growing area in the Shire, beginning with estates such as Vienna Woods. Much of today's housing therefore dates from the late 1960s onwards. The suburb remains strongly detached: in 2021 separate houses made up 93.5% of occupied private dwellings (5,497 of 5,881), with 6,303 private dwellings in total and a median age of 38 (SA2 figures). Pockets of bushland remain, including the 5.6-hectare Windemere Road Bushland Refuge, a koala habitat site, and the Wehr Urban Habitat. The suburb is set back from the bay.

## Wellington Point (QLD 4160)

Wellington Point is a peninsula suburb ending in a point on Moreton Bay, where a sand causeway connects to King Island at low tide. It was a holiday and recreation area for many decades, linked by rail from 1887, and suburban growth was minor until the 1970s. The heritage-listed house Whepstead dates from 1889. In 2021 the area had 4,450 private dwellings, separate houses made up 88.6% of occupied dwellings (3,699 of 4,174), and the median age was 43, older than the state median (SA2 figures). Homes near the point and foreshore are exposed to bay winds and salt air.

## Ormiston (QLD 4160)

Ormiston lies on Moreton Bay between Wellington Point and Cleveland. It is named after Captain Louis Hope's estate: Ormiston House, a heritage-listed brick residence built in stages between about 1858 and 1865, overlooks the bay, and Queensland's first commercially milled sugar was produced at Hope's mill on Hilliards Creek in 1864. Council's open space planning describes Ormiston as mainly residential, with significant open space and conservation land bordering Moreton Bay. Its median age in 2021 was 47, well above the state median. Of 2,390 occupied private dwellings, 75.6% were separate houses (1,807), with 2,565 private dwellings in total (SA2 figures).

## Birkdale (QLD 4159)

Birkdale stretches from Tingalpa Creek, the boundary with Brisbane, east to Wellington Point, and fronts Waterloo Bay. It stayed largely rural, with a large poultry industry, until suburban growth took its population from about 650 in 1961 to more than 5,000 by 1981. The Aquatic Paradise canal estate, one of the Redlands' two canal estates, was begun in 1986 at the mouth of Tarradarrapin Creek. The Birkdale Community Precinct retains about 40 hectares of protected bushland. In 2021 Birkdale had 15,230 residents and 5,800 private dwellings; separate houses made up 84.8% of occupied dwellings (4,632), and the median age was 43 (SA2 figures).

## Mount Cotton (QLD 4165)

Mount Cotton is inland and hilly, well away from the bay. Redland City Council describes it as rural lifestyle country, with residential pockets set among expanses of native bush. Venman Bushland National Park, which protects the headwaters of Tingalpa Creek, is part of the Koala Bushland Coordinated Conservation Area, a network of about 1,170 hectares of linked reserves around Daisy Hill, Sheldon and Mount Cotton. Housing is almost entirely detached: in 2021, 2,277 of 2,287 occupied private dwellings (99.6%) were separate houses. The suburb had 7,302 residents, a young median age of 34, and 2,388 private dwellings in total. Heavy tree cover means more leaf litter and shade than the coastal suburbs.

## Sheldon and Thorneside (QLD 4157 and 4158)

These two neighbouring service areas are very different.

**Sheldon (4157)** is a small rural-residential locality that keeps much of its natural bushland, extending north from Venman Bushland National Park, with reserves such as Emu Street Bushland Reserve and Ford Road Conservation Area. Tingalpa Creek runs through it to the Leslie Harrison Dam, and much of Sheldon is water-supply catchment. In 2021 it had 1,762 residents in 575 private dwellings; 99.1% of occupied dwellings were separate houses, and households averaged 3.1 people.

**Thorneside (4158)** is a small bayside suburb on the north side of the mouth of Tingalpa Creek, named after the railway siding known as Thorne's siding. It has a more mixed housing stock: in 2021, separate houses made up 63.5% of its 1,620 occupied dwellings (SA2 figure), and the median age was 41. Its bay and creek frontage means salt air for homes near the water.

## Brisbane south-east and bayside: Wynnum, Manly and Carindale

**Wynnum (4178)** grew as a bayside holiday destination, and Brisbane City Council's Wynnum Heritage Trail traces that history. The Wynnum, Manly and Lota foreshores form part of the Bayside Parklands, a 16-kilometre ribbon of connected parks along Moreton Bay. In 2021, separate houses made up 71.4% of occupied dwellings (4,051), with townhouses and similar at 12.7% and flats or apartments at 15.9%; the median age was 43 (SA2 figures).

**Manly (4179)** grew as a seaside resort after the railway was extended to the area in 1889; a heritage-listed c.1888 house on Kingsley Terrace is a two-storey timber building with wide verandahs, typical of that era's seaside homes. In 2021 Manly had 4,273 residents and 1,878 private dwellings; separate houses made up 70.6% of occupied dwellings (1,183), and the median age was 46. Both suburbs face directly onto the bay.

**Carindale (4152)** is inland, away from bay salt air. The Carindale housing estate went on the market in 1976 and the suburb was named in 1980, so much of its housing dates from the 1970s onwards. Bulimba Creek runs through it, bordered by extensive open space, about half of which is the Pacific Golf Club. In 2021, 88.7% of its 5,743 occupied private dwellings were separate houses (5,093), and the median age was 43.
