## The climate behind the calendar

The figures below come from the Bureau of Meteorology's Brisbane station (040913), which has records from 1999. They give a clear picture of the year's rhythm.

| February | 176.1 | 9.7 | 30.2 |
| March | 149.2 | 10.0 | 29.2 |

The Redlands tends to be wetter. The Bureau's long-running Redlands station (040265) records a mean annual rainfall of about 1,270 mm, with February and March each averaging more than 160 mm. Its records show mean 9 am humidity above 70 per cent in late summer.

Two things stand out. First, roughly two-thirds of Brisbane's annual rain falls between November and March. Second, July to September is reliably dry, with about four rain days or fewer a month on average. That dry spell is the natural time for exterior work.

## Late winter to early spring (August–September): the main prep window

This is the most useful time of year for exterior maintenance. The weather is dry and mild, roofs dry quickly, and the storm season is still ahead.

- **Gutters, valleys and downpipes.** Leaf litter has had months to build up. Clearing them now means they're ready for the first storms. Get Ready Queensland's advice is to keep gutters and downpipes clear before storm season.
- **Roof cleaning.** Dry, mild days suit roof work. It's also a good time to have a roofer fix any cracked tiles, loose screws, flashing or pointing, as Queensland Government storm guidance recommends.
- **Solar panels.** The dry months are when dust, pollen and smoke settle on panels without regular rain to rinse them. If your monitoring shows a drop, now is when cleaning makes most sense.
- **Tree trimming.** Cut back branches overhanging the roof if you can do so safely, or have an arborist do it.
- **Window tracks and drain holes.** Clear them before heavy rain tests them.

## Storm season build-up (October–December): finish and check

The Bureau of Meteorology describes October to April as Australia's peak severe-weather season, and says the highest thunderstorm risk on the east coast is in northern New South Wales and southern Queensland during October to December. Rainfall starts climbing in October.

- **Finish storm prep early.** Work through Get Ready Queensland's checklists, including securing loose outdoor items. The Insurance Council of Australia also suggests reviewing your policy's exclusions before the season.
- **Photograph clean gutters and the roof.** The Insurance Council of Australia recommends keeping records of maintenance. A dated photo is easy evidence.
- **Look after every big storm.** From the ground, check gutters for overflow marks, downpipes for blockages and the roof for anything dislodged. Leave roof work to professionals, and never climb up while it's wet or windy.
- **Rinse salt and storm grime** off windows and frames if you're close to the bay.

## Wet season (January–March): watch, don't scrub

February is Brisbane's wettest month and March has the most rain days. Heat, rain and humidity together are ideal conditions for mould, algae and moss.

- **Expect growth on shaded surfaces.** South-facing walls, under eaves, decks, paths and fences that stay damp will often go green or black. Queensland's Residential Tenancies Authority points out that heat, humidity and moisture after storms and floods can all drive mould growth.
- **Keep gutters flowing.** Under heavy trees, a quick check between storms can prevent an overflow.
- **Hold off on big roof jobs.** Frequent rain and slippery surfaces make this a poor time for roof cleaning. Book for the dry season instead.
- **Let the rain help.** Heavy summer falls rinse roofs and solar panels naturally. Spot-clean only stubborn deposits such as bird droppings.

## Autumn (April–June): clean up after summer

Rainfall drops by more than half from March to April, and temperatures ease.

- **House washing.** This is a good time to wash off the mould and algae that grew over summer, before it has another season to establish. BlueScope recommends washing sheltered, "unwashed" areas of COLORBOND® steel, such as fascias, eaves and the underside of gutters, at least every six months, or every three months near the coast.
- **Second gutter clean** if you're under deciduous or heavy-dropping trees.
- **Paths, driveways and pool surrounds.** Slippery algae growth on shaded paving is worth tackling before winter.
- **Roof inspection.** If summer storms dislodged anything, autumn gives you time to repair it before the next season.

## Winter (June–July): big jobs and planning

Mild, dry days with low rainfall make winter a reliable time for larger projects.

- **Roof cleaning and restoration** work schedules well around stable weather.
- **Exterior painting prep.** Painters generally want a clean, dry surface, so washing first is worth planning in.
- **Plan spring jobs early.** Late winter fills up quickly with homeowners preparing for storm season.

## Selling your home?

There's no "wrong" season for pre-sale cleaning, but a few points help:

- **Allow lead time.** Book cleaning a week or two before listing photos so any treated growth has time to fade and the house looks its best on camera.
- **Prioritise what buyers see first.** Driveway, entry, front windows and the street-facing roof make the strongest first impression.
- **Dry-season bookings are more predictable.** If you're selling between November and March, build in a buffer for rain delays.
- **Don't hide defects.** Cleaning can reveal cracked tiles, rust or failed pointing. Better to find and fix them before a building inspection does.

## A simple year at a glance

| Season | Priority jobs |
|---|---|
| Aug–Sep | Gutters, roof clean and repairs, solar check, tree trimming, window tracks |
| Oct–Dec | Final storm prep, photo records, post-storm checks, bayside window rinse |
| Jan–Mar | Gutter checks under trees, spot-clean solar if needed, plan dry-season work |
| Apr–Jun | House wash, paths and pool surrounds, second gutter clean, roof inspection |
| Jun–Jul | Larger roof or painting projects, book spring jobs early |

Every property is different. A house under gums near the bay needs more frequent attention than an open block inland. Use the calendar as a starting point, adjust for your trees and exposure, and keep anything involving height for people with the right equipment.
