## Why windows streak

Most streaks and spots come from one of three things:

1. **Minerals in the water.** Tap water contains dissolved minerals. When droplets evaporate on glass, those minerals stay behind as small, cloudy spots.
2. **Cleaning product residue.** Too much detergent, or a product that doesn't rinse cleanly, dries as a film. Australian glass manufacturer Viridian says it does not recommend ammonia-based or alcohol-based glass cleaners because they tend to leave visible streaks.
3. **Dirty frames and tracks.** Grit and grime on the frame get dragged back across the glass by a cloth, squeegee or run-off.

Fixing streaks is therefore less about effort and more about method.

## Two ways to clean exterior glass

### Traditional: wash and squeegee

The familiar method: a strip washer with a little soapy water, then a squeegee to pull the water off before it dries, then a lint-free cloth for the edges. Done well, it gives an excellent finish on glass you can reach safely from the ground or from inside.

Viridian's home glass-care advice suggests a mild solution, such as a commercial glass cleaner or roughly one part vinegar to ten parts water, and warns against scouring products and metal tools.

### Pure-water-fed poles

A water-fed pole system pumps purified water, usually produced by reverse osmosis and deionisation, up a lightweight telescopic pole to a soft brush. The brush agitates the dirt on the glass and frame, then a rinse of pure water carries it away. Because the water has almost no dissolved minerals, it dries without leaving spots, so there's no squeegee and no detergent.

The main advantage is safety. The UK's Health and Safety Executive recommends that window cleaners avoid working at height where reasonably practicable, and names telescopic water-fed poles as one way to do that. Ladders are not banned, but they should not be the automatic first choice.

| | Wash and squeegee | Pure-water-fed pole |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Ground-floor glass, inside panes, small panes | Upper-storey glass, large areas, frames and sills |
| Water | Tap water with a small amount of detergent | Purified water with no detergent |
| Access | Hands on the glass, so ladder work for upper floors | From the ground for most two-storey homes |
| Finish | Excellent when done well, with edges detailed by hand | Spot-free as it dries; needs a full rinse to avoid drips from frames |
| Watch-outs | Ladders, residue, dirty squeegee rubbers | Trailing hoses and wet surfaces underfoot |

Neither method is "better" across the board. Many professionals use both: a pole for upper floors and large expanses, hand work for detail and anything within easy reach.

## Screens and tracks

Clean glass in a dirty frame doesn't stay clean for long. The next shower or wind rinses grime from the frame back down the glass.

Australian aluminium window manufacturer AWS recommends routine clearing of sills, tracks, threshold drains and drain holes as part of ongoing maintenance. Drain holes are how rainwater escapes from sliding door and window tracks, so they are worth checking before storm season.

A simple routine:

- Vacuum tracks with a narrow nozzle, then wipe with a damp cloth.
- Clear drain holes with a soft brush or pipe cleaner. Don't force sharp objects into them.
- Remove fly screens where they're designed to come out, brush them, rinse gently and let them dry before refitting.
- Leave security screens in place unless you know how they're secured, and clean them in position with a soft brush and gentle rinse.

## Living near the bay: salt spray

Properties close to Moreton Bay get a fine coating of salt carried on the wind. It leaves a dull, slightly gritty film on glass and frames that light showers don't fully remove.

How close counts? BlueScope, which makes COLORBOND® steel, classes sites within 100 metres of calm salt water, such as bays and harbours, as "severe marine", and 101 to 200 metres as "marine". It notes that prevailing winds and sheltered areas also affect exposure, so these are guides rather than hard lines.

For aluminium window frames, AWS recommends washing at least every three months where salt air is present, and monthly for beachfront and severe marine locations. Viridian's SmartGlass cleaning guide likewise recommends more frequent cleaning for glass exposed to sea spray.

## Hard-water and sprinkler stains

White, cloudy rings at the bottom of windows near garden beds are usually mineral deposits from sprinklers or run-off.

Glass manufacturer Pilkington explains that, at first, glass marked this way isn't actually stained or etched. It just has a deposit on the surface. But if deposits build up and stay damp against the glass for long periods, staining or etching can follow. Pilkington also warns that using abrasive or strong acid cleaners to remove deposits may work carefully once but, repeated, will ultimately damage the glass. Its advice to homeowners and landscapers is to minimise sprinkler overspray on glass.

What that means in practice:

- **Adjust sprinklers** so they don't hit glass. This is the single most effective fix.
- **Clean fresh marks early**, while they're still a surface deposit.
- **Accept the limits.** Once glass is etched, the damage is in the glass rather than on it, and no amount of cleaning will remove it.

## What not to use on glass

- **Razor blades and metal scrapers.** Viridian warns that fine glass particles from manufacturing can cling to heat-treated (toughened) glass, and dragging a blade across can permanently scratch it. Viridian does not warrant damage caused by razor blades or metal scrapers.
- **Scourers and abrasive pads**, for the same reason.
- **Pressure washers on windows.** Window manufacturer PGT advises against power washers on its windows, and pressure won't remove etching anyway.
- **Squeegees on coated surfaces** unless the manufacturer allows it. Some low-E coated glass is cleaned differently: Viridian's guidance for one coated product says not to use a squeegee on the coated face.

If you're not sure what glass you have, check your builder's handover documents, or treat it as coated and toughened and stick to the gentlest method.

## Doing it safely

Ground-floor glass is a comfortable DIY job. Upper-floor glass is where people get hurt: the Queensland Government reports that more than 1,000 Queenslanders are hospitalised each year after falling off ladders, mostly around the home. A water-fed pole, used from the ground, removes most of that risk.
