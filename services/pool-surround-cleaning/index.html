Pool surrounds

Pool surrounds are wet, shaded in patches and walked on barefoot. They’re the surfaces where organic growth matters most.

How it’s done.

Coping, surrounds and the paving that runs back to the house collect a slick film that’s easy to miss when it’s wet and obvious when it dries. It’s also a genuine slip risk in an area where people are barefoot and moving quickly.

The care here is as much about what we protect as what we clean: keeping run-off out of the pool where practical, working around equipment and fencing, and using a method suited to the coping material.

Why it matters
Salt-water pools splash salt onto surrounding stone. Rinsing coping regularly reduces the salt crystallisation that can make some stone spall.

What’s included

  • Coping material identified and matched to a method
  • Run-off managed away from the pool where practical
  • Pool equipment and fencing worked around, not over
  • Even coverage across the full surround
  • Edges and steps detailed by hand

Surfaces we handle

  • Pool coping and bullnose edging
  • Travertine and stone surrounds
  • Concrete and paver pool decks
  • Adjoining paths and alfresco areas
  • Fence lines and equipment surrounds
Honest limits

We’re exterior cleaners, not pool technicians — we don’t service, drain or chemically balance pools. Some run-off is unavoidable on a tight surround; where that’s the case we’ll flag it before starting so you can plan a filter run.

Pool surrounds, answered.

Common questions about pool surrounds. More in the full FAQ.

Will cleaning affect my pool water?

We keep run-off away from the pool where practical. On tight surrounds some is unavoidable, so we’ll flag it beforehand and you may want to run the filter or check the chemistry afterwards.

Can you make my pool surround less slippery?

Removing the organic film is the biggest single improvement. Slip resistance also depends on the material and finish itself, which cleaning can’t change.

Do you service pools?

No. We clean the surrounds, coping and paving — pool equipment and water chemistry are for a pool technician.
