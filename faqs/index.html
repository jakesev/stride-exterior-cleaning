Working with Stride

Can you quote from photos?

Usually, yes. Most driveways, house washes, gutters, windows and patios can be priced from a few clear phone photos plus your postcode. Roofs, solar, three-storey homes and anything with tricky access normally need a quick free visit first — the quote form tells you which applies before you send.

Do you use high pressure on every surface?

No. High pressure suits sound concrete and most paving. It is the wrong tool for render, painted weatherboard, fibre cement, timber, roof tiles and natural stone, where it drives water behind the surface, lifts coatings and etches soft stone. Those surfaces are soft-washed: a cleaning solution does the work at low pressure, is given time to dwell, then rinsed.

Will cleaning damage my render or paint?

Not when the method matches the material. Sound render and sound paint are unaffected by a correctly applied soft wash. Coatings that are already blistering, chalking or lifting can come away — the cleaning reveals a failure that was already there rather than causing it. We identify that before any work is booked.

Are you insured?

Yes. Stride carries $10 million public liability cover. The certificate of currency is available on request, and body corporates and property managers generally ask to see it before work starts — we’d rather you asked too.

Do I need to be home?

Not necessarily. On the day we need access to the areas being cleaned, an outdoor tap with reasonable flow, and a number to reach you on. Many customers are at work while we’re there. If you want to walk through it with us at the start or end, we’re happy to.

Which marks will not come out?

Cleaning removes what sits on a surface; it does not alter the surface itself. Oil that has soaked into porous concrete, rust bleed, efflorescence, paint spill and UV fade will usually lighten rather than disappear. Etching, spalling and delamination are physical damage. You’ll be told which category a mark falls into before the job is priced.

What happens to the run-off?

In Queensland, waste water from outdoor cleaning is a prescribed water contaminant and must not be allowed into gutters or stormwater drains. We plan where water goes before starting — directing it to lawns and garden areas where appropriate, protecting drains, and keeping chemistry to what the surface actually needs.

How long does a job take?

A standard double driveway is typically around half a day. A full house exterior including eaves is usually a full day. Gutters depend almost entirely on access and tree cover. You get an expected duration and an arrival window with the quote.

How often should exterior surfaces be cleaned?

It’s driven by exposure, not the calendar. A shaded southern wall under trees in a humid bayside suburb needs attention far sooner than an open, north-facing slab. As a working guide: driveways and paths every one to two years, house exteriors every one to three, gutters before each storm season where there is significant tree cover.

Do you provide before-and-after photographs?

Yes, on most jobs — shot from the same position before and after so the comparison is fair. If you’d prefer your property wasn’t photographed, say so and it won’t be.

Which areas do you service?

The Redlands is home ground: Redland Bay, Victoria Point, Cleveland, Thornlands, Capalaba, Alexandra Hills, Wellington Point, Ormiston, Birkdale and Mount Cotton. Selected work is taken across bayside and south-east Brisbane depending on scope. The quote form checks your postcode instantly.

How do I book?

Two ways. Get a free quote first (recommended if you haven’t used us before), or request a booking directly by choosing the services and two or three preferred dates. Either way, a real person confirms the scope, price and arrival window before anything is locked in.

Surfaces & methods

Is soft washing the same as pressure washing?

No. Pressure washing uses the force of high-pressure water to remove dirt. Soft washing applies a cleaning solution at low pressure, leaves it to work for a set time, then rinses it off gently. Soft washing suits surfaces that force can damage, such as render, painted weatherboard, fibre cement and roof coatings. Pressure washing, set to suit the slab, is generally used on sound concrete.

Sources: Soft washing — Wikipedia; Maintenance — Washing (Code of Practice) — NZ Metal Roofing Manufacturers

Can I pressure wash my rendered walls?

Only gently, and only if the render is sound. Rockcote's maintenance guide specifies about 500 psi through a fan tip, not a needlepoint jet, which it warns can lift even the sturdiest finish. Natural lime and clay renders are more delicate. Rockcote says to avoid high-pressure sprays and bleaches on those altogether. Check for cracking or flaking before any water goes on.

Sources: Rockcote Maintenance Guide — Rockcote; Natural Materials Maintenance Guide — Rockcote

Will pressure washing damage my roof tiles?

It can. Monier strongly advises against using pressure washers to clean roofs. It also notes that walking on tiles risks damage, and recommends leaving roof work to a qualified professional because of the fall risk. Coated roofs have their own limits: Dulux's Acratex roof membrane guide allows less than 450 psi through a fan jet held at least 30 cm away.

Sources: Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier; Acratex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux

What are the black spots on my driveway?

Usually organic growth such as algae, mould or lichen rather than dirt. These grow best in damp, shaded, poorly drained spots, which is why they cluster under trees and along shaded sides of the house. The Royal Horticultural Society notes they don't damage the surface, but they do make it slippery. Lichen is generally the most stubborn and can take more than one treatment.

Sources: Algae, lichens, liverworts and moss on hard surfaces — RHS; What causes those black spots on concrete driveways — SlashGear

Why does the mould come back after cleaning?

Cleaning removes the growth, but not the shade and moisture that let it grow. Discussing lichen on cladding, the NZ Metal Roofing code of practice warns that "recolonisation is very likely", especially in sheltered and shady areas. Trimming overhanging branches, improving drainage and keeping debris swept away slow regrowth. On decks, the Queensland Government suggests removing plants that permanently shade the surface.

Sources: Maintenance — Washing — NZ Metal Roofing Manufacturers; Maintaining decks and balconies — Queensland Government

Can oil stains be removed from a concrete driveway?

Often partly. Concrete is porous, so oil soaks in, and speed matters. Fresh spills respond much better than old ones. For older stains, a poultice (an absorbent paste left on the stain to draw oil out) is commonly used, sometimes over several rounds. Deep, long-standing oil stains usually lighten rather than disappear completely.

Sources: How to remove stains from concrete — The Home Depot; How to remove oil stains — Concrete Network

What is the white powder on my pavers, and how do I get rid of it?

It's usually efflorescence: salts carried to the surface by moisture and left behind as the water evaporates. BRANZ notes it doesn't affect the performance of concrete or masonry. Don't hose it, because water re-dissolves the salt and it returns as the surface dries. Think Brick Australia recommends brushing it off with a stiff, dry brush once the surface has dried.

Sources: BU593 Efflorescence — BRANZ; Manual 13: Clay Masonry Cleaning Manual — Think Brick Australia

Should I seal my driveway after it's been cleaned?

It depends on the surface and your priorities. Sealers can slow staining, help hold exposed-aggregate stones in place and refresh coloured concrete. They also add a reapplication cycle (Concrete Decor puts acrylic-sealed exposed aggregate at roughly 12–36 months), can make surfaces slipperier, and poorly bonded film sealers can lift under hot tyres. Only seal a clean, fully dry surface.

Sources: Seal exposed aggregate concrete for best results — Concrete Decor; Choosing decorative concrete sealers — For Construction Pros; What causes hot-tire pickup? — Concrete Decor

Can I use vinegar or acid cleaners on travertine or limestone?

No. Travertine, limestone and marble are calcareous stones, made mainly of calcium carbonate, and acids dissolve it. Even mild acids like vinegar can etch the surface, and sealing doesn't prevent etching. Use a pH-neutral cleaner made for natural stone, a soft brush and low-pressure water. If you're not sure what your stone is, treat it as acid-sensitive.

Sources: Protecting your investment in Mother Nature with TLC — TileLetter; What stones are most prone to etching — Slabwise; The dos and don'ts for sandstone — Sandstone World

Is it OK to pressure wash a timber deck?

It's risky. High pressure, narrow tips and close spraying can strip soft wood between the grain, leaving a fuzzy "furred" surface or permanent marks that need sanding. If pressure is used, keep it low, use a wide fan tip, keep your distance and work with the grain. A deck cleaner and stiff brush with a gentle rinse is usually safer. The Queensland Government recommends sweeping instead of hosing where possible.

Sources: How to power wash a deck without damaging your wood — Olympic; Pressure-washing a deck — HGTV; Maintaining decks and balconies — Queensland Government

How often should I re-oil my deck in Queensland?

On condition, not by the calendar. Cabot's suggests inspecting every 12 months for loss of colour or water repellency. Intergrain recommends recoating before the coating breaks down, and three coats in full sun, near pools or in humid areas. Rules of thumb vary: RACV suggests every six to eight months for oil-based coatings. If water soaks in instead of beading, it's time.

Sources: Natural Decking Oil — Cabot's; UltraDeck Timber Decking Oil — Intergrain (Bunnings listing); Deck cleaning, restoration and repair — RACV; Tips and tricks for timber deck maintenance — Canterbury Timbers

Will house washing chemicals hurt my plants, pets or rainwater tank?

They can if no one plans for them. Sodium hypochlorite, common in soft-wash solutions, can damage plants and is hazardous to eyes and skin. Plants should be wetted before, during and after, and pets kept inside until surfaces are rinsed. Disconnect downpipes feeding a tank before roofs or gutters are washed, as BlueScope and Dulux advise, and keep runoff out of stormwater drains.

Sources: Sodium Hypochlorite Safety Data Sheet — Dalcon Hygiene; How to minimise plant damage when using sodium hypochlorite — Pure Seal Services; Home Improvement Maintenance Guide — BlueScope; Acratex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux; Water pollution — Redland City Council

Roofs, gutters, solar & windows

How often should I clean my roof in Brisbane or the Redlands?

There's no fixed rule. It depends on shade, nearby trees, roof material and how close you are to the bay. Many roofs only need attention every few years, when streaking, lichen or moss becomes noticeable or gutters keep filling with debris. Coated (restored) roofs are different: Dulux recommends washing its AcraTex roof coatings about every 12 months, and more often in coastal areas. Monier suggests a professional roof inspection roughly every five years.

Sources: Dulux AcraTex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux (copy hosted by Vivify Roofing); Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier

Can I pressure wash my roof tiles?

It's not recommended. Monier, a major Australian roof-tile manufacturer, strongly advises against using pressure washers to clean roofs. High pressure can damage tile surfaces, coatings and ridge pointing, and force water under laps and flashings. Dulux warns that heavy staining on its roof coatings should never be removed with a high-pressure blaster. A low-pressure method matched to the roof material is the safer choice.

Sources: Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier; Dulux AcraTex Roofing Maintenance Guide — Dulux (copy hosted by Vivify Roofing); AcraTex roofing care and warranty — Dulux

Are the black streaks and lichen on my roof doing damage?

Usually they're mostly cosmetic at first. Some manufacturers note that moss and lichen don't eat into tiles, but heavy growth holds moisture, slows run-off and sheds debris into gutters and valleys. It can also hide cracked tiles or failed pointing. Light growth on a sound roof isn't urgent. Heavy growth, blocked gutters or a leak are good reasons to have the roof cleaned and inspected.

Sources: Concrete roof tiles — Marshalls; How do I remove moss or lichen from my roof? — Marley; Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier

Does my Colorbond roof need washing?

Mostly not. BlueScope says surfaces regularly washed by rain, which includes most roof sheeting, don't usually need routine washing. Areas sheltered from rain do: fascias, the underside of eave gutters, wall cladding under eaves, carports and patios. BlueScope recommends washing those at least every six months, or every three months in coastal locations with salt spray, using fresh water and a soft approach.

Sources: Maintain COLORBOND® steel and ZINCALUME® steel — BlueScope; Home Improvement Maintenance Guide — BlueScope

When should I clean my gutters in South-East Queensland?

At minimum, once a year in late winter or early spring, before storm season. The Bureau of Meteorology identifies October to December as the highest thunderstorm-risk period for southern Queensland, and Get Ready Queensland advises keeping gutters and downpipes clear of leaves and blockages. Homes under gums or heavy canopy may need cleaning two or more times a year. Monier recommends at least yearly, more often with surrounding trees.

Sources: Prepare now for Australia's severe weather season — Bureau of Meteorology; Home maintenance and preparation — Get Ready Queensland; Roof cleaning and maintenance — Monier

If I install gutter guards, do I still need to clean my gutters?

Yes, though less often. Guards stop most leaves getting in, but fine grit, seeds and pollen can pass through or collect on the mesh, and leaves can mat on top and shed water over the edge in heavy rain. Valleys, box gutters and outlets still need checking. Get Ready Queensland's advice to keep gutters and downpipes clear applies whether or not guards are fitted.

Sources: Home maintenance and preparation — Get Ready Queensland; Preparing for the tropical cyclone season — Blue Mountain Mesh

Will my insurance cover water damage from blocked gutters?

It depends on your policy and the circumstances. Insurers generally cover sudden events, not damage from poor maintenance. A 2023 Code Governance Committee review found 55 per cent of the denied home claims it examined relied on wear-and-tear or maintenance exclusions. The Insurance Council of Australia recommends clearing gutters, fixing roof damage and keeping records of maintenance work.

Sources: CGC concerned by denied home insurance claims trends — Insurance Business Australia; Insurance Council: 264,000 claims in 2025 but many denied — The Senior; Gutter mess: landlord loses mould claims dispute — Insurance News

Doesn't rain clean my solar panels?

Often it does, especially in heavy summer falls. Research notes that in high-rainfall locations, rain can keep panels sufficiently clean. But light showers may not count: soiling models used by the IEA's solar program treat rain as cleaning only above a daily threshold, such as 5 mm. Flat-mounted panels, bird droppings, salt and the dry July–September months are where soiling builds up.

Sources: Soiling and natural cleaning of PV modules (EPJ Photovoltaics, 2026) — EPJ Photovoltaics; Fact sheet: Soiling (Task 13) — International Energy Agency PVPS; Climate statistics for Australian locations: Brisbane (040913) — Bureau of Meteorology

How much power do dirty solar panels lose?

Usually less than people think, unless soiling is concentrated. An International Energy Agency report summarised in Australian media put typical soiling losses at around 3 to 5 per cent globally, higher in dusty regions. Bird droppings are a bigger concern: a small 2024 study found droppings covering about 30 per cent of a panel cut output by about 35 per cent. Check your monitoring data before paying for a clean.

Sources: Dirty panels are cutting output from solar PV by up to five per cent — RenewEconomy; Effect of bird droppings on PV panel performance (Sukkur IBA Journal of Emerging Technologies, 2024) — DOAJ

Can I clean my solar panels with a hose and detergent?

A gentle hose rinse from the ground, early in the morning, is generally fine. Avoid detergents unless your panel manual allows a specific product. Manufacturers such as Jinko warn against high-pressure cleaning, Trina advises against alkaline or strong acid solvents, and Trina says damage from inappropriate cleaning can void its warranty. Deionised water and a soft brush are the standard recommendation. Never spray cold water on hot panels.

Sources: Operation and maintenance manual — JinkoSolar (copy hosted by Memodo); Vertex Series user manual — Trina Solar; Quick guide: cleaning — Midsummer

Why do my windows have white spots that won't come off?

They're usually mineral deposits from sprinklers or hard water. Glass maker Pilkington explains these start as surface deposits but, if they stay damp against the glass for long periods, can stain or etch it. Abrasive or strong acid cleaners may help once but, repeated, will damage the glass. Redirect sprinklers away from windows and clean fresh marks early. Etching is in the glass and can't be cleaned off.

Source: ATS-126: Guidelines when using lawn sprinklers — Pilkington

Are water-fed poles as good as traditional window cleaning?

For exterior glass, they're a well-established alternative. A water-fed pole uses purified water, which has its minerals removed so it dries without spots, with no squeegee or detergent needed. The UK's Health and Safety Executive names water-fed poles as a way to avoid working at height when cleaning upper-floor windows. Hand cleaning with a squeegee still gives an excellent finish on glass that's easy to reach.

Sources: Working at height whilst window cleaning — Health and Safety Executive (UK); Window cleaning services using pure water — Designing Buildings Wiki

Booking and quotes

Should I get more than one quote?

It can help, as long as the quotes describe the same job. Compare which surfaces are included, the method for each, whether run-off control is included, and any exclusions. Consumer guidance distinguishes an estimate (a rough idea of cost) from a quote (generally a firm price that can be binding once accepted as part of a contract), so ask for written quotes rather than estimates.

Source: Quotes and estimates — Northern Territory Government

What's the difference between a quote and an estimate?

An estimate is a rough idea of what the job might cost and can change. A quote is generally a fixed price for the described work, and once accepted it can form part of a binding contract, so the price shouldn't rise unless the scope changes by agreement. If a business gives an estimate, ask what could change the final price and how you'll be told before extra work is done.

Source: Quotes and estimates — Northern Territory Government

What should a written quote for exterior cleaning include?

A useful quote lists each surface to be cleaned, the method for each, what is excluded, how run-off will be managed, the price (and whether GST is included) and payment terms. Run-off matters because in Queensland waste water from outdoor cleaning is a prescribed water contaminant and must not be allowed into gutters or stormwater drains.

Sources: Quotes and estimates — NT Government; Environmental Protection Regulation 2019, Sch 10 — Queensland Legislation

Someone knocked on my door offering to clean my driveway today. Is that allowed?

Door-to-door selling is legal but regulated. For uninvited sales worth $100 or more, sellers may only call between 9 am and 6 pm on weekdays and 9 am and 5 pm on Saturdays, not on Sundays or public holidays. They must leave immediately if asked, and a "Do Not Knock" sign counts as a request to leave. Queensland residents can order a free Do Not Knock sticker from the Office of Fair Trading.

Sources: Telemarketing and door-to-door sales — ACCC; Do not knock signs — ACCC; Don't get stormed by door knockers — Qld Government

Can I cancel a job I agreed to on the doorstep?

Yes. An agreement that came from an uninvited approach (worth $100 or more) has a 10 business day cooling-off period, and you can cancel for any reason without being charged. During that period the trader generally cannot take payment or start work. The ACCC has recommended changes to these rules, so check its website for the current position.

Sources: Telemarketing and door-to-door sales — ACCC; Rules when selling door to door — Qld Government

Should I pay a deposit before the work is done?

For door-to-door offers, Queensland's Office of Fair Trading advises paying nothing up front, not even for materials, and the law generally prevents payment during the cooling-off period. For work you have invited, practices vary; ask what any deposit covers and get a receipt with the business's full name and street address. Fair Trading has reported travelling traders taking deposits and not returning.

Sources: Don't get stormed by door knockers — Qld Government; Bitumen bandits back in Rockhampton — Qld Office of Fair Trading

How can I check a cleaning business is legitimate?

Ask for the business name and ABN and check it on ABN Lookup. Get a written quote showing a street address, not just a first name and mobile number. Ask for a current certificate of currency for public liability insurance and check the name matches. Fair Trading also suggests noting the vehicle registration of door-to-door traders.

Sources: ABN Lookup — Australian Business Register; Don't get stormed by door knockers — Qld Government; Types of business insurance — business.gov.au

Who do I contact if a trader overcharges me or won't leave?

Queensland's Office of Fair Trading takes reports on 13 QGOV (13 74 68). If a trader refuses to leave your property or demands more money than agreed, Fair Trading advises calling the police. Keep any paperwork, receipts and notes such as the trader's name and vehicle registration.

Sources: Bitumen bandits back in Townsville — Qld Office of Fair Trading; Don't get stormed by door knockers — Qld Government

Preparation

What should I do before the cleaners arrive?

Common preparation includes moving cars off the driveway, shutting windows and doors near the work, bringing pets inside, moving outdoor furniture, pot plants and doormats (or asking whether the cleaner will), and making sure gates are unlocked and an outside tap is accessible. If a fish pond, delicate plants or a pool are near the work area, point them out at the start.

Will the cleaner use my water?

Mobile cleaners commonly connect to the property's outside tap, so ask in advance. In South East Queensland, region-wide water restrictions would begin if combined dam levels fall to 50% of capacity; Seqwater's July 2026 update did not report restrictions. If you're booking in a dry spell, check current status with Seqwater or your water retailer.

Sources: Water restrictions — Qld Government; Water Security Update, July 2026 — Seqwater

Should I tell my neighbours?

It's courteous, especially on shared driveways, close boundaries or where water could flow onto their property. Overspray can reach cars and windows, and run-off that crosses into a neighbour's yard or the street gutter is a problem. In Queensland, letting outdoor cleaning water reach a roadside gutter or stormwater drain is an offence, so the cleaner should plan where water goes.

Sources: Water pollution — Redland City Council; Prescribed water contaminants guide — Noosa Shire Council

Do I need to be home during the clean?

Not always. Many jobs can be done without you if there is access to the work areas, gates and an outside tap, and pets are secured. Being home at the start helps you confirm the scope, point out anything fragile, and agree where run-off will go. Being available at the end lets you check the work before the cleaner leaves.

Safety

Is it safe to clean my own second-storey walls or gutters?

Working at height is a significant injury risk. Product Safety Australia, citing national hospital data, reports that more than 6,200 people were hospitalised after ladder falls in the year to June 2022, and WorkSafe Queensland issued a 2026 alert after a worker died falling from a ladder while accessing a residential roof. If you do it yourself, consider ground-based tools such as extension poles before using a ladder.

Sources: Ladders safety guide — Product Safety Australia; Worker sustains fatal injuries after falling from a ladder — WorkSafe Qld

What should I know about power lines when cleaning?

WorkSafe Queensland sets exclusion zones around overhead power lines and warns that a shock can occur without touching a line, because being close can cause a flashover or arc. Ladders, long metal poles and water spray near the service line to the house need particular care. For work close to lines, the network operator (Energex in South East Queensland) can advise.

Source: Exclusion zones — WorkSafe Queensland

How should a professional work at height on my home?

Businesses must manage fall risks under work health and safety law, and WorkSafe Queensland's guidance treats ladders as a lower-order control to be used only after safer options are considered. It's reasonable to ask how a cleaner will reach upper eaves, walls or gutters, whether from the ground with poles, from a platform, or with ladders secured and supervised.

Sources: Worker sustains fatal injuries after falling from a ladder — WorkSafe Qld; WorkSafe Queensland

Are the cleaning products safe for my family?

Ask which products will be used, by name, and follow the cleaner's advice on keeping children and pets away from treated areas until they've been rinsed and dried. Products used for soft washing can affect skin, eyes, plants and painted finishes if misused. If anyone in the household has sensitivities, mention it when booking.

Pets, plants and the garden

Will exterior cleaning harm my garden?

It shouldn't if run-off is managed. Queensland council guidance allows water carrying only soil and organic matter to be directed onto lawns or gardens, but water containing chemicals or oil must be collected and disposed of properly. Ask how the cleaner will protect plants from chemical-bearing water and overspray, and whether they pre-wet or rinse nearby plants.

Sources: Water pollution fact sheet: mobile cleaning — Somerset Regional Council; Water pressure cleaning — Tablelands Regional Council

Is exterior cleaning safe for pets?

Keep pets inside or away from the work area while cleaning is under way and until surfaces have been rinsed and are dry. Pets can drink from puddles or walk through wet areas and then lick their paws. Ask the cleaner which products they use and tell them about any pets, water bowls or animal enclosures near the work.

What about my fish pond or swimming pool?

Point them out before work starts. Chemicals and dirty wash water shouldn't enter a pond or pool, and Queensland regulations treat chemicals and outdoor cleaning waste water as water contaminants. Covering or shielding a pond, and keeping run-off and overspray away from pool water, are reasonable things to ask about.

Sources: Environmental Protection Regulation 2019, Sch 10 — Queensland Legislation; Water pollution — Ipswich City Council (LG Toolbox)

Can the dirty water just go onto my lawn?

Often, yes, if it contains only soil and organic matter: Queensland council guidance allows that water to be directed onto grass or garden where it can soak in. Water containing oil or chemicals is different and needs to be collected for proper disposal or, with the water authority's approval, discharged to sewer. Some councils suggest using holding tanks.

Sources: Water pollution fact sheet: mobile cleaning — Somerset Regional Council; Water pressure cleaning — Tablelands Regional Council; Trade waste fact sheet — Redland City Council

If a cleaning product is biodegradable, can it go down the stormwater drain?

No. Queensland's Environmental Protection Regulation 2019 lists waste water from outdoor cleaning as a prescribed water contaminant in its own right, regardless of the product used. It is an offence under section 440ZG of the Environmental Protection Act 1994 to let it enter a roadside gutter or stormwater drain, whether deliberately or not.

Sources: Environmental Protection Regulation 2019, Sch 10 — Queensland Legislation; Prescribed water contaminants guide — Noosa Shire Council

Timing and weather

When is the best time of year for exterior cleaning in Brisbane and the Redlands?

Any time can work, but weather affects scheduling. Bureau of Meteorology records for the Redlands station show January to March as the wettest months (about 160 mm each on average) and August and September the driest (about 52 mm and 38 mm). Drier months mean fewer rain delays; in the wet season, growth on shaded surfaces tends to return faster.

Source: Climate statistics: Redlands HRS (040265) — Bureau of Meteorology

Can exterior cleaning be done in the rain?

Light rain doesn't always stop work, but heavy rain or storms usually mean rescheduling. Rain dilutes cleaning solutions, makes surfaces and ladders slippery, and makes it harder to control where water flows, including keeping it out of stormwater drains. With an average of around 47 rain days a year at the Redlands station, expect some flexibility in booking dates.

Sources: Climate statistics: Redlands HRS — BoM; Water pollution — Redland City Council

Should I wait until after storm season?

It depends on your goal. Cleaning before the wet season can clear debris and growth before it builds up; cleaning after can tidy storm mess. Either way, be wary of storm-season door knockers: Queensland's Office of Fair Trading has warned about travelling traders offering cheap on-the-spot work after storms.

Sources: Don't get stormed by door knockers this storm season — Qld Government; Climate statistics: Redlands HRS — BoM

Are there water restrictions on pressure cleaning in South East Queensland?

Not at present, based on the most recent Seqwater update reviewed (July 2026). Under Seqwater's water security program, region-wide restrictions would begin when combined dam levels fall to 50% of capacity, with tighter levels below that, and restrictions can limit outdoor water use. Check Seqwater or your water retailer for the current status.

Sources: Water restrictions — Qld Government; Water Security Update, July 2026 — Seqwater; SEQ water security — Business Queensland

How long before selling should I have the exterior cleaned?

Many sellers book cleaning in the week or two before listing photos, so surfaces look their best without growth returning. In the wet summer months, shaded areas can start darkening again quickly, so closer timing helps. Allow a buffer for rain delays and for dealing with anything cleaning reveals, such as flaking paint.

Source: Climate statistics: Redlands HRS — BoM

Frequency

How often should a house exterior be washed?

There's no fixed rule. Frequency depends on shade, nearby trees, exposure to salt air and how much the home's appearance matters to you. In a summer-wet climate like the Redlands (about 1,270 mm of rain a year on average), shaded and damp walls grow mould and algae faster than sunny ones. Many owners clean when growth or staining becomes visible rather than on a calendar.

Why does my house near the bay get dirty faster?

Airborne salt is deposited most heavily close to the shoreline and decreases inland. On sheltered surfaces, such as under eaves and verandahs, rain doesn't wash salt off, so it accumulates. Salt film dulls glass and paint and can hold moisture and dirt. Bayside homes may need cleaning more often than similar homes further inland.

Sources: AS/NZS 2312.1 guide — Dulux Protective Coatings; Marine atmosphere corrosion report — DTIC

How often should a driveway be cleaned?

It varies with shade, tree cover, vehicle use and how much rain the area gets. Shaded concrete in a wet summer can go green within months, while a sunny, open driveway may stay presentable much longer. Cleaning when slipperiness or staining becomes noticeable is a practical approach; slippery paths are a safety issue, not just a cosmetic one.

Cost

Why do exterior cleaning quotes vary so much?

Quotes often describe different jobs. Price depends on surface area, access, height, how dirty the surfaces are, the materials involved, run-off control and travel. A lower quote may cover fewer surfaces, use one method for everything or leave out run-off management. Compare quotes line by line before comparing totals.

Source: Quotes and estimates — NT Government

Why does a two-storey home cost more to clean?

Height changes equipment and safety requirements, not just effort. Businesses must manage fall risks, and WorkSafe Queensland treats ladders as a lower-order control to be used only after safer options are considered. Safer methods for upper eaves, walls and gutters, such as platforms or extended pole systems, take more equipment and set-up time.

Source: Worker sustains fatal injuries after falling from a ladder — WorkSafe Qld

Should run-off control be included in the price?

Yes, it should be part of the job. In Queensland, outdoor cleaning waste water must be kept out of gutters and stormwater drains, and councils such as Redland City Council name high-pressure cleaning businesses among those that must prevent it. Collecting or containing water takes time and equipment, so a quote that leaves it out isn't comparable with one that includes it.

Sources: Water pollution — Redland City Council; Environmental Protection Regulation 2019, Sch 10 — Queensland Legislation

DIY versus professional

Do the stormwater rules apply to homeowners cleaning their own property?

Yes. The offence of letting a prescribed water contaminant reach a roadside gutter or stormwater drain applies to anyone, not only businesses. Redland City Council suggests residents direct water from vehicle washing onto a vegetated area and keep sediment, mulch and rubbish contained, and the same approach suits DIY exterior cleaning.

Sources: Water pollution — Redland City Council; Depositing contaminants fact sheet — Cassowary Coast Regional Council

Should I hire a pressure washer and do it myself?

For an accessible concrete driveway or path, many homeowners do. The main risks are damaging softer surfaces such as render, paint and timber with too much pressure, working at height, and run-off reaching the street gutter. If the job involves ladders, roofs, power lines nearby or delicate surfaces, a professional with the right equipment and insurance may be the safer choice.

When is it worth paying a professional?

Common reasons include second-storey or roof-level work, large areas, delicate or painted surfaces, stubborn stains, and properties where run-off is hard to control, such as a driveway that falls straight to the street. A professional should also carry public liability insurance, which responds if their negligence injures someone or damages property.

Sources: Types of business insurance — business.gov.au; Worker sustains fatal injuries after falling from a ladder — WorkSafe Qld

Damage, insurance and what's included

What happens if a cleaner damages my property?

Raise it with the business straight away, with photos. Public liability insurance covers a business when its negligence causes injury or property damage, but it isn't compulsory for all occupations, so ask for a current certificate of currency before work starts. If you can't resolve a dispute, the Queensland Office of Fair Trading can provide information on your options.

Sources: Types of business insurance — business.gov.au; Bitumen bandits back in Townsville — Qld Office of Fair Trading

Can cleaning reveal damage that was already there?

Yes. Dirt and growth can hide paint that is chalking or lifting, cracked render, worn sealer or loose pavers, which may become obvious once cleaned. A careful cleaner will look for these before starting and mention them. If repairs or painting follow, check licensing: the QBCC requires a licence for building work over $3,300, and painting and decorating is a licensed trade class.

What is usually included in a "house wash"?

It varies between businesses, which is why the written quote matters. Some include walls, eaves, fascia and downpipes; others add windows, screens, gutters (outside faces) or paths. Ask for a list of included surfaces and exclusions, such as windows, roof surfaces or stain removal, so you can compare like with like.

As a tenant, do I have to pressure clean before I move out?

Not automatically. Under Queensland's Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008, a tenant must leave the premises, as far as possible, in the same condition as at the start of the tenancy, fair wear and tear excepted. The entry condition report is the benchmark. Check your tenancy agreement for special terms about outdoor areas.

Sources: Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008 — Queensland Legislation; Residential Tenancies Authority

In a rental, who is responsible for exterior cleaning?

Under Queensland tenancy law, the tenant must keep the premises clean, having regard to their condition at the start of the tenancy, while the lessor must keep the premises in good repair and fit to live in. Routine tidiness generally sits with the tenant; repairs and larger maintenance generally sit with the owner. Special terms in the agreement can clarify specifics, such as gutters or pools.

Sources: Residential Tenancies and Rooming Accommodation Act 2008 — Queensland Legislation; Residential Tenancies Authority
